Do You Have a Leak?

The tell-tale signs

A pool drops about 2 cm a week to evaporation. More than that, or any of these, and it's worth a test.

Water level drops more than 2–3 cm a week
The auto-filler runs constantly
You're topping up with a hose every few days
Soggy patches, or soil washing away near the pool
Cracks in the shell, or loose or lifting tiles
Air bubbles in the pump basket or at the return jets
The paving or deck is lifting, cracking or settling
Water bill up, chlorine disappearing faster than usual

Try the bucket test first

Stand a bucket on the top step, fill it with pool water, and mark the level inside the bucket and the pool level outside it. Leave both 24 to 48 hours with the pump running as normal. If the pool drops more than the bucket, water is escaping. Run it again with the pump off: if the pool loses less with the pump off, the leak is in the plumbing; if it's the same either way, it's the shell, skimmer, lights or fittings. Either way, that's where we start.

How We Find It

Four ways to pin it down

We use whichever the pool needs, and usually more than one, until the source is located to within centimetres.

Pressure testing

We isolate each plumbing line, pressurise it with air, and watch the gauge. A line that won't hold pressure is a line with a leak — and now we know which one.

Acoustic location

Water escaping a pressurised pipe makes a sound. With ground microphones we follow that signature and mark the spot above the leak, so any digging is a small, exact hole.

Dye testing

For the shell, skimmer throat, light niches and fittings — a fine stream of dye near a suspect spot gets drawn straight into a leak and shows us the path.

Dive & shell inspection

A close inspection of the main drain, returns, light housings and any structural cracks, in the water, checking the parts you can't see from the edge.

Why It's Worth Doing

A slow leak rarely stays small

  • Thousands of litres and rands of water lost over a season.
  • Chemicals draining away with the water, so dosing costs climb.
  • Water under the shell can undermine the pool and the ground around it.
  • Wet soil moves, and moving soil cracks paving, decks and sometimes foundations.
  • Found early it's a small repair. Left alone it becomes a big one.

Good To Know

Common questions

How do I know it's a leak and not just evaporation?

The bucket test settles it. Evaporation takes water from the bucket and the pool at the same rate; a leak takes extra from the pool. If the pool drops noticeably more than the bucket over a day or two, you have a leak.

Do you have to break the pool or dig it up to find the leak?

No. The whole point of pressure testing, acoustic location and dye testing is to find the leak without damage. If a repair needs a small excavation, we mark the exact spot first so it's one small hole, not a trench.

How long does a leak test take?

Most tests are done in a single visit of a couple of hours. Awkward or multiple leaks can take longer, and we'll tell you if a return visit is needed.

Can you find leaks in the underground plumbing?

Yes. Pressure testing tells us which buried line is leaking, and acoustic equipment tracks it to a spot on the surface above the pipe.

My pool only loses water when the pump is running. What does that mean?

That points to the pressure side of the plumbing — the return lines. Water only leaks out while it's being pushed through. It's a useful clue and it's exactly what pressure testing confirms.
